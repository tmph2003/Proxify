# Chapter 9: Usability testing on 10 cents a day

## Core Idea
Usability testing isn't an expensive luxury; it's a basic requirement that you can and should do cheaply and often.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **DIY Usability Testing**:
  - When to use: At least once a month, for a morning.
  - How: 
    1. Recruit 3 users (anyone, doesn't need to be your target audience).
    2. Give them a scenario and ask them to complete tasks on your site or wireframes.
    3. Ask them to "think aloud" as they work.
    4. Watch, listen, and take notes. DO NOT help them or explain things.
    5. Debrief with your team immediately after and fix the most severe problems.

## Key Concepts
- **Focus Group vs. Usability Test**: Focus groups get people's opinions about what they *think* they want (good for marketing). Usability tests watch one person at a time *try to use* something (good for design).
- **Thinking Aloud**: The core of a usability test. The user verbalizes their thought process as they navigate.

## Anti-patterns
- **Waiting until the site is "done" to test**: You will find problems that are too late/expensive to fix. Test sketches, test wireframes, test competitors' sites.
- **Fixguing over user demographics**: For most usability flaws (e.g., "the button is invisible"), you don't need a domain expert. Any user will stumble on it.
- **Trying to fix everything**: You will find dozens of problems. Only fix the top 3 most severe ones before the next test.

## Key Takeaways
1. Testing one user is 100% better than testing zero users.
2. A test is just watching someone try to do tasks while thinking aloud.
3. You can test your site, a prototype, or even a competitor's site.
